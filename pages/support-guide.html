Support others with respect and confidence

Simple, practical guidance for allies who want to create accessible, welcoming spaces for people with disabilities.

Offer support Learn more

Why your help matters

The best support comes from people who listen, learn, and act with care.

When you offer help the right way, you make everyday life easier and more respectful for people with disabilities. AssistAbility is about building trust, not just offering assistance.

Be the ally people want

Ask before you help

Never assume someone needs assistance. A simple question like “Would you like help?” shows respect and gives people choice.

Use people-first language

Say “person with a disability” rather than labels. Focus on the person first and their ability second.

Listen and follow their lead

If someone explains how to support them, follow that guidance. Their needs and preferences matter most.

Talk, ask, then help

Speak clearly

Use a normal tone and clear words. For people with hearing loss, face them and keep your mouth visible.

Ask what they prefer

Ask if they want help, and if so, how. For example, “Would you like me to read this for you?” or “How can I support you best?”

Respect personal space

Do not move a person’s wheelchair, cane, or service animal without permission.

Everyday ways to help

  • Offer to describe an image or environment when someone asks.
  • Give people extra time when they need it.
  • Share accessible resources and tools — captions, large text, or sign language help.
  • Stand up for inclusive access in your community.

Ready to offer support?

You can make a real difference by learning, listening, and acting with kindness.
